GULAB JAMUN



Description

Gulab jamun is a popular, sweet dessert from the Indian subcontinent made of soft, deep-fried milk dumplings soaked in a fragrant sugar syrup.

Ingredients

  • Milk Solids: Traditionally made with khoya or mawa (milk reduced to a soft dough). Modern or quick recipes often use milk powder instead. Some authentic recipes also mix in a small amount of fresh paneer (cottage cheese) for a softer texture.
  • Flour: A small amount of all-purpose flour (maida) or semolina (sooji) is added to the dough to hold the ball together during deep frying.
  • Leavening: A pinch of baking soda is often included to keep the inside light and spongy.
  • Frying Medium: The balls are fried in ghee or cooking oil.

Steps in makeing Gulab Jamun

  1. Make the Sugar Syrup
    • Combine the sugar and water in a wide saucepan over medium heat
    • Add the crushed cardamom pods and saffron strands
    • 1. Stir until the sugar completely dissolves, then let it simmer for about 6 to 8 minutes. The syrup should feel slightly sticky, like honey, but without forming any string consistency.
    • Turn off the heat and stir in the rose water and lemon juice. Cover and keep the syrup warm.
  2. Prepare the Dough
    • In a large mixing bowl, sift or mix together the milk powder, all-purpose flour, and baking soda.
    • Add the melted ghee and rub it into the dry mixture using your fingertips until it resembles coarse crumbs.
    • 1. Pour in warm milk one tablespoon at a time. Gently bring the mixture together into a soft, smooth dough. Do not over-knead the dough, or the jamuns will turn out dense and hard.
    • Cover the dough with a damp cloth and let it rest for 5 to 10 minutes
  3. Shape the Balls
    • Pinch small portions of the dough (about the size of a small marble or 15 grams each)
    • Roll each portion firmly between your palms until it is completely smooth and free of cracks. Cracks will cause the balls to break apart when frying. Cover the shaped balls with a damp cloth to prevent them from drying out.
  4. Fry the Gulab Jamuns
    • 1. Heat ghee or oil in a deep pan over low-to-medium heat. The oil should not be too hot; test a tiny piece of dough—it should rise slowly and gently without browning instantly.
    • Gently drop the balls into the oil in small batches to avoid overcrowding.
    • Continuously and gently swirl the oil around the pan so the balls roll and brown evenly on all sides to a deep golden-brown color. This takes about 3 to 5 minutes per batch.
  5. Soak and Serve
    • 1. Remove the fried jamuns with a slotted spoon and let them cool for 1 to 2 minutes.
    • 1. Transfer them directly into the warm (not boiling hot) sugar syrup.
    • Let them soak covered for at least 1 to 2 hours so they double in size and absorb the syrup completely.
    • Serve warm or at room temperature.
